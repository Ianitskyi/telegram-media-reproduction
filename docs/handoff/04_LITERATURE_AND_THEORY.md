# Literature and theoretical frame

## Core classical works

### Gatekeeping
- White, D. M. (1950). “The ‘Gate Keeper’: A Case Study in the Selection of News.”

Use as historical baseline for newsroom control over selection.

### Agenda-setting
- McCombs, M. E., & Shaw, D. L. (1972). “The Agenda-Setting Function of Mass Media.”

Use to describe classic media power over issue salience, not as the direct mechanism measured here.

### Two-step flow / opinion leaders
- Katz, E., & Lazarsfeld, P. F. (1955). *Personal Influence*.

Highly relevant. Use as the classic model of mediated information flow: messages can reach audiences through intermediaries/opinion leaders. Do **not** claim Telegram channels are literally identical to classic opinion leaders. Frame them as platform intermediaries / secondary distributors that can perform a comparable relay/interpretation function.

## Digital/platform theory

### Ambient journalism
- Hermida, A. (2010). “Twittering the News: The Emergence of Ambient Journalism.”

Use to explain fragmented, continuous information flows and news encountered outside complete article packages.

### Hybrid media system
- Chadwick, A. (2013). *The Hybrid Media System*.

Central frame: old/new media logics interact rather than replace each other. Strong fit with the article’s core argument.

### Distributed news / source attribution
- Kalogeropoulos, A., Fletcher, R., & Nielsen, R. K. — work on news brand attribution in distributed environments (verify exact year/title in final bibliography; project drafts contain both 2018 and 2019 references).

Core relevance: users often consume journalism outside the publisher’s own environment and may not remember the originating brand. Supports the distinction between content origin and source visibility.

### Incidental news exposure
- Boczkowski, P. J., Mitchelstein, E., & Matassi, M. (2018), “News comes across when I’m in a moment of leisure”.

Useful to explain platform-based news exposure where users encounter news without actively seeking a media brand.

### Affordances / uses and gratifications
From Gordiienko’s bibliography, potentially useful:
- Sundar & Limperos (2013), Uses and Grats 2.0
- Sundar (2008), MAIN model
- Bucher & Helmond (2018), platform affordances
- Hutchby (2001), affordances
- Treem & Leonardi (2013)
- Leonardi & Vaast (2017)

Use these to explain why Telegram is attractive as a news environment, not as the main theory of content origin.

## Telegram-specific and Ukraine-relevant work

### Gordiienko (2025)
Use for:
- Telegram in wartime/crisis communication;
- speed, convenience, locality, perceived control;
- theoretical bibliography on affordances and uses/gratifications.

Also inspect its full reference list for adjacent theoretical work before finalizing the literature review.

### Steblyna & Vasylyk (2026)
Work on Ukrainian Telegram channels as alternative/citizen/amateur media.

Key relevance: popular Telegram channels are difficult to classify simply as traditional media; they are often anonymous/non-institutionalized, but remain interlinked with mainstream media.

### Knuutila et al. (2020)
“Junk news distribution on Telegram” / work on source distribution in Telegram.

Relevant as a direct methodological/literature predecessor for tracing links/news sources in Telegram.

### Herasimenka et al. (2022)
“Misinformation and professional news on largely unmoderated platforms: the case of Telegram.” Verify exact author list and DOI in final bibliography.

Useful because it compares circulation of professional news and misinformation on Telegram, but is not Ukraine-specific and is more link/source oriented.

### Ptaszek, Yuskiv & Khomych
“War on frames…” — computational/text-mining analysis of war coverage on Telegram. Verify exact bibliographic details/DOI before use.

### UIMC (2023)
Report/research on how non-institutionalized news Telegram channels in the Ukrainian segment operate and gain audience.

Important contextual source for defining the non-institutionalized Telegram ecosystem.

### Tonkikh (2023), Dosenko (2023)
Potentially useful Ukrainian works on Telegram news content during wartime. Verify bibliographic details and relevance before citation.

### Hoseini et al. (2024)

### Ostrovska & Lyashkevych (2026)
“How Ukraine Reads the War: a Five-Year Dataset of 20 Telegram News Channels…”

Important caveat for novelty: do **not** claim this project is the first longitudinal Ukrainian Telegram dataset. Novelty should instead focus on tracing professional-media content reproduction across multiple detection methods.

## Proposed theoretical chain

A coherent narrative:

**White / McCombs & Shaw** → classic centralized media selection and agenda power

**Katz & Lazarsfeld** → information reaches audiences through intermediaries

**Hermida** → continuous fragmented platform news flow

**Chadwick** → hybrid media system, mutual interdependence of old/new actors

**Kalogeropoulos et al.** → distributed news and weaker source-brand attribution

**Telegram / affordances literature** → why the platform became a major news environment in Ukraine

**Current study** → empirically tests whether professional journalism remains embedded in popular Telegram flows even when source attribution is weak or absent.

## Research gap formulation

A defensible working version:

> Попередні дослідження добре описують зростання ролі Telegram в Україні, специфіку анонімних і неінституціоналізованих каналів, використання Telegram професійними медіа та державними акторами, а також циркуляцію професійних новин або дезінформації на платформі. Водночас бракує довготривалих кількісних досліджень, які б визначали, яку частку контенту популярних немедійних Telegram-каналів можна простежити до професійних українських медіа не лише через прямі посилання або згадки, а й через семантичне відтворення тексту, та як ця частка змінювалася до і після початку повномасштабного вторгнення.

Do not claim absolute novelty until a broader literature search is completed.
